The problem

Weather apps predict tomorrow.
A digital twin answers "what if?"

India's monsoon decides food (kharif sowing), water and heat for over a billion people.

"Physics-aware AI is the backbone of climate adaptation and policy, from disaster preparedness to energy grid planning." (organisers' brief)

Our twin: 32 × 36 grid of 1° cells, 6 variables (temperature, rain, wind u/v, pressure, water vapour).

Judged on: forecast skill, heavy rain, sharpness (anti-blur), and shock causality: no teleporting, no averaging out.

How it works

One small, local step, repeated

Ensemble of 10, rollouts averaged
6 plain members (reach 6 cells/day) + 4 wind-advection members (semi-Lagrangian carry along the 10 m wind, ≤ 2 cells/day, then a CNN residual; reach 8). A shock's response comes from one member.
Trained on what is scored
metric-aligned loss: error ÷ best baseline, the judge's own skill ratio; unrolled 1 → 2 → 4 → 8 days

Honest limits

What it can't do (yet)

  • Day 8 ≈ climatology. Day 1 is strong; late leads are not.
  • Monsoon-stall shock propagates only 39% of the time: energy stays near the box on the grid's western edge.
  • No ocean model: a warm-sea anomaly fades in ~4–5 days.
  • 1° daily means can't resolve cyclone cores.
  • Tried and rejected: damping toward climatology, all-years retrain. Both cost causality.

Evidence for each point, with how it was measured: docs/EXPERIMENTS.md
