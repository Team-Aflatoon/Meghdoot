Repo: github.com/Team-Aflatoon/Meghdoot · python serve.py --port 8000 (offline, CPU) · every number below: docs/EXPERIMENTS.md

USP — shocks travel like weather, by construction. In Kalidasa's Meghadūta a monsoon cloud carries a message across India. Our simulator carries any shock across India the same way: it starts where it was applied and moves at most 8 grid cells per simulated day. The network contains only small convolutions (no pooling, attention or spatial normalisation), so its reach per daily step is fixed, and a test proves it bit-exactly. A zero shock returns exactly the unshocked timeline (max |Δ| = 0).

1. Model design

  • Anomaly space. The net sees departures from a 365-day climatology (15-day smoothed) and predicts tomorrow's change of the anomaly. Forecasts relax toward climatology at long leads instead of drifting.
  • Local stencil. A 96-channel stem plus 5 residual blocks reaches 6 cells per step. Padding is replicate. The output head starts at zero, so an untrained net equals anomaly persistence.
  • Physics-aware member. Two of the 5 members first carry the anomaly along the 10 m wind (semi-Lagrangian, bilinear, learned scale, capped at 2 cells/day) and learn only the residual. Their CNN has 4 blocks, so reach is still 8.
  • Ensemble of 5 (3 plain, 2 advection; different seeds). Each rolls out separately, then the rollouts are averaged.
  • Training. Unrolled curriculum on the model's own predictions: 1 → 2 → 4 → 6 days (30 epochs, AdamW, cosine LR, grad-clip 1). Loss is latitude-weighted MSE in std units, ×4 weight where observed rain > 10 mm/day. 7–15 min per member (RTX 4050).
  • Honest validation. The shipped members are trained on 2005–2019 only and scored on held-out 2020–2021 with the kit's own monsoon_r1.scoring functions, so every number below belongs to the shipped model. We tried retraining on all years and damping toward climatology; neither improved the measured shocks, so neither ships.
  • Judge replica. Offline Docker (4 CPU, 6 GB, read-only): kit validator passes, 0.3–0.5 s per call, 0.2 GB; repo scan 15/15.

4. Failure modes we know about

  • Monsoon-stall shock. The response is non-zero, local and stable, but most of its energy stays within 3 cells of the Arabian Sea box (on the domain's western edge). The judge's "propagates outward" check failed in 6/6 held-out windows, costing about 1.9 points. The warm-Bay shock passes all four checks in the 6 summer/cyclone windows, and propagates in 89% of 72 windows across all seasons (weakest in winter).
  • Rain is the weakest channel (skill ≈ −0.015 vs the best reference; CSI is mostly earned at day 1). At 1° and daily means, convective rain is nearly unpredictable past a few days. Also: the ensemble mean is smoother than reality at day 8+, there is no ocean model, and 2023–24 is warmer than training.

5. Visualisation: the Multiverse dashboard (dashboard/, separate from the server)

Static HTML/JS with no build step and no CDN, so it runs offline. Every map comes from a live /simulate call to our server, and a red banner shows if the server is unreachable.

  • Six real scenarios: Cyclone Tauktae, Cyclone Yaas, Kerala onset 2021, Konkan extreme rain July 2021, winter western disturbance, and a heatwave. Pick a channel, play days 1–30, and toggle observed reality and wind arrows.
  • Three panels: Timeline Prime, Multiverse and Δ = Multiverse − Prime (diverging scale, white = 0).
  • Shock painter: drag a box on the map (it snaps to 1° cells) and stack components (+K, ±%, mm/day, hPa). The official presets "Warm Bay of Bengal" and "Monsoon stall" are included. "Zero shock" demonstrates max |Δ| = 0 exactly.
  • Spread chart: response energy near vs far from the box per day, plus the furthest cell reached. It shows the bounded-speed spread live, which is the hard-to-copy proof judges can poke at.

6. Impact extension: Heat & Kharif water-stress co-pilot

Audience: state agriculture and disaster-management cells who must decide, a week ahead, whether a stalled or weakened monsoon threatens kharif sowing, or whether a heat spell is coming. Question it answers: "If the monsoon flow weakens / the Bay warms, which regions lose how much rain and gain how many heat days over the next 10 days?"

Regions are 4 approximate boxes: NW India, Central, Peninsular South, East & NE. The co-pilot is a decision aid built on our simulator, not an official forecast. A real deployment would use IMD district boundaries and daily-max temperature.

7. Demo script (5 min)

  1. 0:00–0:40 Cyclone Yaas start state: play Prime and toggle Observed. 2021 was never seen by the shipped model, so this is a genuine out-of-sample forecast.
  2. 0:40–2:00 Warm Bay of Bengal: on day 1 the Δ is confined to the box; by days 4–8 it spreads to the coast; the spread chart's reach grows with no jump.
  3. 2:00–2:40 Zero shock: "max |Δ| = 0 (exactly)". Then the judge paints any shock they like.
  4. 2:40–4:00 Monsoon stall, then the co-pilot table and advisories for Central and Peninsular India.
  5. 4:00–5:00 Architecture slide: anomaly stencil, ensemble, held-out numbers, honest failure modes.

8. Rules compliance

  • Offline at inference: no weather APIs, no dates in inference code, no data archive in the repo (the 365-day climatology is allowed, and the repo scan confirms it).
  • Weights are in the repo (5 × 1.9 MB plus climatology), and training code is included.
  • The server imports no UI library. The dashboard is a separate folder and is always driven by live /simulate output.
  • All code and weights were created during the hackathon. Libraries: PyTorch, NumPy, FastAPI, xarray (training only).
